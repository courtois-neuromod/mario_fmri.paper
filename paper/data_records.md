# Data Records

The paired *mario* and *mariostars* datasets are distributed by the Courtois
NeuroMod project under a CC0 waiver and are organised following the Brain Imaging Data Structure
(BIDS) {cite:p}`gorgolewski2016bids`
(Data Citation 1); access instructions are at <https://docs.cneuromod.ca>. Per-subject
gameplay totals are summarised in {numref}`Table %s <tab-behavior>`, and dataset coverage and annotation
statistics are shown in {numref}`Fig. %s <fig-overview>`. The five participants contributed 120
participant-sessions and 484 runs to *mario*, compared with 51 participant-sessions and 182
runs to *mariostars*. These records contain 3,374 and 1,232 level repetitions,
respectively, corresponding to 84.0 and 31.2 hours of gameplay ({numref}`Fig. %sA,B <fig-overview>`). All 22
levels are represented in both tasks, although the ordered discovery protocol produced greater
and more uneven level-wise sampling in *mario* ({numref}`Fig. %sC <fig-overview>`).

**Raw gameplay and events (`mario/`).**
Each participant folder contains, per run, a BOLD acquisition and, under `gamelogs/`, the
artefacts of every repetition: the `.bk2` replay, an `.mp4` recording, a
`_summary.json` of game outcomes, a `_variables.json` of per-frame emulator RAM
(including player position and button states), a `_lowlevel.npy` of low-level
audiovisual features, an `_events.tsv` of repetition timing, and a richer
`_desc-annotated_events.tsv` of the frame-accurate button-press, in-game, and replay
events described in Methods. End-of-run FSS-2 and affect responses are stored as
questionnaire events.

**Derivatives (`mario.fmriprep/`, `processed/`).**
fMRIPrep outputs (preprocessed BOLD in `MNI152NLin2009cAsym` space, brain masks, and
confound tables) are provided under `mario.fmriprep/`. Analysis outputs generated by the
`mario_fmri` package — session- and subject-level beta/$z$-maps, MVPA results,
beta-map correlations, and behavioral learning-trajectory tables and figures — are written to
`processed/{analysis}/`, each with a JSON provenance sidecar and, where applicable, a
BIDS `dataset_description.json`.

**Acquisition, annotation, and behavioral coverage of the *mario* and
*mariostars* datasets.** Data are shown for the same five participants in both tasks
(`sub-01`, `sub-02`, `sub-03`, `sub-05`, and `sub-06`).
(**A**, **B**) Acquisition timelines for *mario* and *mariostars*,
respectively. Horizontal position is cumulative run count across sessions; each block is one
run, gaps separate sessions, and the red triangles in A mark the transition from ordered
discovery (light red) to randomized practice (dark red). Labels at right report the number of
sessions (s), runs (r), and gameplay hours (h) for each participant. (**C**) Mean number
of repetitions per participant and level for *mario* (red) and *mariostars*
(blue). Solid and hatched portions denote cleared and failed repetitions, respectively; error
bars show $\pm 1$ SD of total repetitions across participants ($n=5$). (**D**) Distributions across sessions of
annotation counts for six controller actions and four in-game events, shown separately for
each participant on a logarithmic axis. Split violins compare *mario* (brick red, left half)
with *mariostars* (deep navy, right half). (**E**) Corresponding session-wise
distributions of median press duration for the four sustained controller actions, in seconds
on a logarithmic axis. (**F**–**H**) Level-matched behavioral trajectories for
cleared-repetition rate (F), median score expressed as a percentage of the level-specific
maximum (G), and median time to clear among cleared repetitions (H). Each line represents one
participant. Repetitions were ordered separately within each participant, phase, and level and divided
into early, middle, and late thirds; metrics were first computed per level and then averaged
across levels so that levels contributed equally. The first two three-bin sections of each
panel show *mario* discovery and practice, and the final, navy-shaded section shows
*mariostars* practice; the time-to-clear axis in H is reversed so that improvement is upward.
All panels show empirical behavioral data.
:::
